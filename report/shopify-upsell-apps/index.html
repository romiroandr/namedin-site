Method

How to reproduce every number here.

Nothing on this page is modelled or estimated. Each figure is a count over one table, and the table has one row per pair of question and cited source.

  • 15 questions, written the way a merchant types rather than the way a keyword tool suggests, in three shapes: problem first, with a constraint, and head to head.
  • Run 2026-08-25, one clean session per question, no follow-ups, no personalisation carried between them.
  • Retrieval-based answers only. Every answer here ran a live web search and cited pages. That is what is measured: which pages the search surfaced and what they said.
  • Recorded as 102 rows — 5 to 8 cited sources per question, 6.8 on average. Product names and domains are transcribed exactly as returned.
  • Owner assigned to every domain by opening it: who publishes it, and whether they sell a product in this category, next to it, or not at all.

What this does and does not measure

This is search-grounded answering, not the model's memory.

When an assistant searches the web before answering, its names come from the pages it just read — so this report is really a measurement of those pages. Answers produced from a model's own training data alone are a different surface, they move far more slowly, and nothing here should be read as describing them.

One run per question, one category, one platform, one date. Retrieval results shift week to week; treat every number as a snapshot of 2026-08-25, reproducible by asking the same 15 questions again and writing down what comes back.

Who owns the sources

52 of 102 citations point at a page published by a competing app.

That is 51% of everything the answers read in this category, spread across 24 of the 58 domains. It is a measurement, not an accusation: writing a "best apps" list that includes your own app is ordinary content marketing, and it long predates AI answers. What is new is that a retrieval-based answer treats that page as a source and passes its shortlist along.

Three things the breakdown says plainly.

  • Every one of the 15 questions drew on at least one page published by an app in the category. There was no question where the answer read only neutral sources.
  • Official Shopify pages accounted for 9 citations across 3 domains, appearing in 9 of the 15 answers — present, but a minority of what was read.
  • Independent editorial — blogs, review sites and industry writing with nothing to sell here — accounted for 11 of 102 citations. The thin part of the picture is not vendor content; it is everything else.

Reading it

The answer is downstream of the page.

In this category, a retrieval-based answer is close to a summary of the 5 to 8 pages the search returned. That explains most of what the tables show: a product named on many of those pages is named in many answers, and a product on none of them has no route into the answer at all — however old it is, however many merchants use it, however it is rated, unless a page the answer reads says so.

It also explains why a shortlist can turn over quickly. Nothing here is a ranking of quality, and nothing here is stable: change the pages, and the same 15 questions return a different list.
